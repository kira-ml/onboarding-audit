# Red-Team Audit — V1

**Status:** V1 adversarial audit, 2026-11-21.
**Subject:** `onboarding-audit` v0.2.1.
**Method:** Inversion and falsification: state the claim, try to disprove it, and
report what survives.
**Independence:** This is a **self-audit by the author**. It is weaker than an
independent review and is labeled as such. An independent adversarial pass is
recorded in §7.

---

## 1. The claim under audit

Two claims are often conflated. They have different evidence.

**Narrow claim (what the repository can support):**

> On a public synthetic account-opening dataset, under a stated and simulated
> observation mechanism, naive policy evaluation is biased and can reverse the
> ranking; a doubly robust estimator with known propensities recovers the
> `P0` point estimate but not always the ranking; an audit is the only
> approximately unbiased option when approved labels are outcome-dependent; and
> the reject margin is not point-identified.

**Broad claim (what it cannot support):**

> The method will produce a valid policy-value estimate on a specific bank's log.

The broad claim needs a partner log, an independent validator, and a
pre-registered protocol. It is **not** claimed.

## 3. Findings

Findings are ranked by severity. Status: **fixed**, **disclosed**, or **open**.

### F1 — The empirical evidence is a simulation (critical, disclosed)

Every measured number is generated by an observation mechanism the authors chose
(`e_approve`, `kappa_review`, label delay, audit rate). The "naive bias
7.8–538.5%" is a property of that grid, not an estimate of a bank's bias. A
hostile reviewer can say: "You simulated a biased mechanism and then measured the
bias."

**What survives:** the *conditional* statement (given this mechanism, the naive
estimator is biased and the ranking can reverse) and the *method* (the gate, the
estimators, the reproducible pipeline). The magnitude does not transfer.

**Fix:** none available without a partner log. The disclosure must stay in the
first paragraph of every summary.

### F2 — `P0` is not the candidate policy's value (high, fixed)

The primary estimate excludes logging-rejected applications, but the candidate
policy may change their treatment. Printing a "policy value" that omits the rows
the candidate changes most is an overclaim.

**Fix applied:** the memo now says **`P0`-conditional policy value**, always
reports the candidate actions on logging-rejected rows, and adds a
**full-policy value: not identified** row. (The first implementation read a
non-existent key and printed 0; the independent pass caught it and it is fixed
with a regression test — see §7.1 I5.) **Residual:** a reader can still quote
the `P0` number as the policy value; the label is the only defense.

### F3 — The gate cannot test the assumption that matters most (critical, partially fixed)

The documented worst failure is outcome-dependent approved labels (DR bias up to
1,627.90%). A log can pass every statistical check and still have that mechanism.
Before this audit, the memo would say "Measured" without asking.

**Fix applied:** the report now requires an observation-mechanism declaration.
`outcome` refuses; `unknown` (the default) marks the memo **"Measured
(observation assumption unverified)"**; `action` marks it **"Measured"**.

**Residual:** an analyst can declare `action` falsely. The gate cannot verify the
declaration. This is a governance control, not a statistical test.

### F4 — False precision in the sweep (medium, fixed)

The sweep has 50 replications per cell. The docs quoted "64%" and "84.5%" to
0.1–1 percentage point. The Wilson 95% interval for DR P(best) at low `e` is
**[50%, 76%]**; for coverage at low `e`, **[78.8%, 89.7%]**. One-point
differences between cells are noise.

**Fix applied:** the sweep summary reports Wilson intervals; the results doc
states that the direction is solid and the decimals are not. **Residual:** the
coverage interval treats policy–replications as independent, so it is an
optimistic lower bound.

### F5 — The bounds fail their own gate (high, disclosed; partially resolved)

F5 (`coverage_full >= 90%`) holds in **15 of 88** cells for the narrow interval
and **77 of 88** for the widened interval; the main scenario is **34–87%** narrow
and **99–100%** widened. The 11 widened failures are exactly the cells where the
`P0` estimate is biased.

**What survives:** the widened interval is a valid (conservative) 95% confidence
interval under the identification assumptions, and it passes F5 except where
those assumptions fail. The narrow interval is not a confidence interval. The
honest conclusion is that the reject margin needs a controlled design, not a
better narrow interval.

**Residual:** the narrow interval is still printed. A reader may treat it as a
confidence interval despite the label.

### F6 — The cost matrix is assumed (high, disclosed)

`fraud_loss_rate = 1 / mean(train amount) = 0.0019187869` is a normalizer, not a
peso loss-given-fraud. The review cost, false-reject cost, and residual loss are
normalized assumptions. The ranking is cost-sensitive.

**Fix applied:** the derivation is documented. **Residual:** a real institution
must supply its own matrix; the pilot cannot validate it.

### F7 — Reproducibility is one machine (medium, partially fixed)

The benchmark rebuild matched the recorded SHA-256 exactly (scikit-learn 1.7.2,
lightgbm 4.7.0). A different library version can change the scores. CI cannot run
the benchmark because it needs the 213 MB raw file.

**Residual:** the provenance sidecar records versions; the hash is the check. A
reviewer on another stack may see a mismatch and must report it, not hide it.

### F8 — The audit is a self-review (medium, open)

The author built and audited the work. The tests and the reproducible pipeline
reduce the risk of self-deception, but they do not replace an independent
validator. The pilot protocol requires one; V1 does not have one.

### F9 — Overclaiming language (medium, fixed)

"Decisive" for the audit, despite 80–86% coverage; "35% coverage" after the
rerun produced 34%; "policy value" for a `P0`-conditional number. All corrected
in the current tree.

### F10 — Underpowered cells (medium, disclosed)

Some sweep cells cannot distinguish 84.5% from 85.0% coverage. The claims that
survive are the large contrasts (0% vs 64–100% ranking; 7.8% vs 538% bias), not
the fine ones.

## 4. What is genuinely new

The audit should also record what survives, or it is only destruction.

1. **Accurate point estimate ≠ correct ranking.** At the lowest approve-label
   rate, DR's point estimate is within ~1.5%, but it selects the true best policy
   only 64% of the time (95% CI [50%, 76%]). Most evaluation papers report the
   first number and imply the second.
2. **The audit is conditionally valuable, not universally.** In the main
   action-dependent scenario, a 1% audit improves DR RMSE by ~8%. Under
   outcome-dependent labels, it is the only approximately unbiased option. The
   practical rule is: audit when approval labels are outcome-dependent, not as a
   default.
3. **The reject margin is a design problem, not a statistics problem.** No
   amount of weighting identifies it from logs alone; it needs a controlled
   approval design or an explicit assumption. The narrow interval fails its
   coverage gate; the widened interval is valid but wide. That is the honest
   result.
4. **The failure mode is detectable before the estimate.** A gate that asks for
   the observation mechanism, the label counts, the ESS, and the cost support
   catches most unidentified logs before a number is printed.

## 5. Verdict

| Claim | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| The repository is internally consistent, reproducible, and honestly scoped | **Pass (conditional)** | 86 tests, exact benchmark hash, claim ledger, gate, disclosed limitations |
| The simulation results hold as stated, conditional on the simulated mechanism | **Pass (conditional)** | Pre-registered sweep, Wilson intervals, Monte Carlo bounds, F5 split recorded |
| The method will work on a specific bank's log | **Inconclusive** | No partner log, no independent validator, cost matrix unvalidated |
| The reject margin is point-identified from logs alone | **Fail** | The narrow interval fails F5; the widened interval is valid but 4× wider and fails where `P0` is biased |

**Overall:** **PASS for the Level 2, simulation-scoped claim; INCONCLUSIVE for
any production or generalization claim.** INCONCLUSIVE is not safe: do not
present the simulation numbers as evidence about a specific institution.

## 7. Independent adversarial pass

Two independent agents reviewed the tree: an adversarial audit of the claims and
a correctness review of the estimator code. Both found defects the self-audit
missed. The claim audit returned **FAIL on the claims as stated** — not because
the mechanism is wrong, but because the precision ("DR within 1%", "widest
0.009942", "12/12") and two gate/memo behaviours were not supported by the
artifact. Those defects are fixed or disclosed below; the corrected claims are in
`CLAIMS.md` and `RESULTS.md`.

| I2 | "12/12 cells >5%" is the maximum over four policies; the incumbent is **1.52%** at `e050_k050`. | High | **Fixed**: per-policy claim (incumbent 11/12) |

and the "IPW fails with estimated `e`" result (a propensity bug). The sweep's

per-scenario forms. The independent pass moved the audit from a self-review to a
review with external findings; it did not find a leakage, a baseline, or a
reproduction failure.

## 8. What would change the verdict

- A partner log and an independent validator reproducing the result → moves the
  production claim from INCONCLUSIVE to E3/E4.
- A narrow confidence-interval construction for the reject margin that passes F5
  → moves the bounds claim from FAIL to conditional.
- Evidence that the observation mechanism in a real log is action-dependent and
  correctly declared → strengthens the main claim.
- A demonstration that the gate passes a log with a falsely declared mechanism →
  reopens F3 as a hard fail.
